Drawing from a multidisciplinary background in microbiology, microbial ecology, evolutionary biology, and bioinformatics, I am broadly interested in understanding how microorganisms adapt, evolve, and function across diverse and extreme environments. My current research focuses on the comparative and functional genomics of cold-adapted bacterial lineages isolated from Arctic and Antarctic environments. Using genome-resolved bioinformatics approaches, I investigate the conservation, diversity, and evolutionary significance of associated genes, with particular emphasis on photosynthetic reaction center proteins, bacteriochlorophyll biosynthesis pathways, cold-adaptation, and carbon metabolism.

Through comparative analyses integrating KEGG, Pfam, BLAST-based annotation, and phylogenomics, I aim to understand how genomic adaptations support energy acquisition, ecological fitness, and bipolar distribution in cold marine ecosystems. This work contributes to a broader understanding of microbial adaptation, metabolic flexibility, and the ecological roles of phototrophic bacteria in polar environments. Beyond the lab, I leverage this expertise to mentor students and support capacity building in microbiology and bioinformatics, particularly by guiding students through microbiology-focused dissertation projects in under-resourced contexts.

Ultimately, I am passionate about fostering inclusive scientific collaboration between institutions in the Global North and South. My long-term goal is to contribute toward strengthening global research infrastructure, delivering robust interdisciplinary training, and ensuring equitable access to genomic and microbiological sciences.
